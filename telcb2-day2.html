01

Introduction

Today we look at the first scored part of the speaking exam: Über Erfahrungen sprechen — talking about an experience.

You choose one topic from a fixed list of seven. You speak about it for about 90 seconds. Then your partner asks you a few questions. Later you do the same for your partner: you listen to their talk and you ask them questions. Both parts get a score.

Many teachers and YouTube videos call this part „die Präsentation“. That is only a nickname. The official telc name is Über Erfahrungen sprechen. This name is important. This is not a formal presentation with numbers and structure. It is a personal story.

Note about the warm-up. Before this part, the examiners ask you to introduce yourself a little (Einander kennenlernen / Kontaktaufnahme). In telc B2 this warm-up is not scored. Do not mix this with telc B1. In B1, introducing yourself is a scored task.

Why this part is important

  • It is 25 of the 75 speaking points — one third of your speaking score.
  • The speaking block must reach 60% by itself. That means 45 of 75 points. A good written score cannot save a weak speaking score.
  • It is the first picture the two examiners get of your German. A calm, fluent start helps them hear everything after it in a better way.
  • Unlike Parts 2 and 3, you control the content. You already know the seven possible topics.
02

What you will learn today

At the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  1. Name all seven possible topics, and the small points that come with each.
  2. Build a five-part plan for a 90-second talk. It works for any topic.
  3. Write a keyword note sheet (Stichwortzettel) instead of a full text.
  4. Ask your partner good follow-up questions — and answer theirs.
  5. Understand the four things examiners score, and what each one rewards.

Today’s goal, in one sentence

Leave with one prepared talk for each of the seven topics, and a fixed plan you can put any story into.

You get 20 minutes to prepare before the speaking exam. You sit in a separate room. You get the task sheets and paper for notes. Those 20 minutes must cover all three speaking parts. You can prepare Part 1 at home. So use only 4–5 minutes for it in the exam room. Give the rest of the time to Parts 2 and 3.

You usually take the speaking exam in pairs (sometimes groups of three). There are two examiners. The examiners mostly lead the exam. They are not your conversation partner. Your partner is the other candidate.

The seven topics

The list grew from five to seven topics in October 2019. Old books may still show only five. This is the current list. Each topic has small points you should talk about.

01
Eine Reise
A trip
Place, when, the country and its people, sights, why you still remember it.
02
Ein Buch
A book
Theme, author, what it is about, your opinion, who you'd recommend it to.
03
Ein Film
A film
Plot, actors, genre, your opinion, what impressed you.
04
Ein Sportereignis
A sports event
Which sport, where, who was there, the result, the atmosphere.
05
Eine Musikveranstaltung
A music event
Genre, musicians, venue, audience, how you felt.
06
Eine wichtige Person im Leben
An important person in your life
Who, how you met, when, why they matter to you.
07
Eine wichtige Erfahrung
An important experience
What, when, where, with whom, why it was important.

Prepare all seven. Do not hope you will get your favourite. Seven talks of 90 seconds sounds like a lot. But they share the same plan, and about half of the same phrases. In real life it is one evening of work, plus repeating.

The five-part plan

Every talk, for any topic, has the same shape:

1 · 10s
Einstieg — say the topicName your topic. In one short sentence, say why you chose it. This gives you a calm start.
2 · 20s
Rahmen — set the sceneWhen, where, with whom, how old you were. Clear facts. No opinion yet.
3 · 30s
Kern — the main storyWhat happened. What was hard. What surprised you. The details live here.
4 · 25s
Bewertung — why it was importantYour opinion, with a reason. Many people skip this. But this is what the task really asks for.
5 · 5s
Abschluss — finishOne last sentence so everyone knows you are done.

Steps 1, 2 and 5 are almost only fixed phrases. Only steps 3 and 4 change with the topic. That is why seven talks is less work than it sounds.

A model talk — Eine wichtige Erfahrung

1 · Einstieg

Ich möchte euch heute von einer Erfahrung erzählen, die mein Leben ziemlich verändert hat: von meinem ersten Auslandssemester in Wien.

2 · Rahmen

Das war vor ungefähr vier Jahren, im Herbst. Ich war damals 21 Jahre alt und hatte vorher noch nie länger als zwei Wochen allein gelebt. Ich bin also mit zwei Koffern angekommen und kannte in der ganzen Stadt keinen einzigen Menschen.

3 · Kern

Am Anfang war es ehrlich gesagt ziemlich hart. In der Universität habe ich zwar alles verstanden, aber im Alltag habe ich mich oft nicht getraut zu sprechen. Besonders schwierig war für mich, dass ich abends allein in meinem Zimmer saß und niemanden hatte, mit dem ich reden konnte. Nach etwa einem Monat bin ich dann einem Laufverein beigetreten — und plötzlich ging alles viel schneller.

4 · Bewertung

Rückblickend würde ich sagen, dass diese Zeit die wichtigste Erfahrung meines Lebens war. Ich habe nämlich nicht nur die Sprache gelernt, sondern vor allem, dass ich mich auf mich selbst verlassen kann. Das hat mir seitdem in vielen Situationen geholfen.

5 · Abschluss

Deshalb kann ich jedem nur empfehlen, einmal für längere Zeit ins Ausland zu gehen. — Das war meine Erfahrung. Hast du dazu Fragen?

In English: the speaker talks about their first semester abroad in Vienna, at age 21. The first month was lonely. Then they joined a running club. At the end they say the experience taught them to trust themselves. Notice: about one third of the talk is opinion, not events.

You may invent the story. Nobody checks if you really went to Vienna. If a true story is hard in German, tell a simpler story that you can tell well. Fluency gets points. A true biography does not.

Notes, not a full text

If you read full sentences from paper, your voice sounds flat. Examiners hear this at once. Your notes should look like this:

WIEN — Auslandssemester
vor 4 J. · Herbst · 21 J. alt · 2 Koffer · niemand gekannt
Anfang hart → Uni ok, Alltag nicht · abends allein
nach 1 Monat → Laufverein → alles schneller
Bewertung: wichtigste Erfahrung · nicht nur Sprache, sondern auf mich selbst verlassen
Schluss: jedem empfehlen → ins Ausland

Keywords only. Look at your partner and the examiners, not at the paper.

The four scoring points

All three speaking parts use the same four scores. Each part is 25 points:

What they scoreWhat it rewards
Ausdrucksfähigkeit
Range of language · 7 pts		 Different words and sentence shapes. Do you have more than one way to say something? Do you use B2 connecting words, not only und / dann?
Aufgabenbewältigung
The task and fluency · 7 pts		 Did you really do the task — tell the story and say why it was important? Did you speak long enough, without long pauses? Did you answer your partner’s questions?
Formale Richtigkeit
Grammar · 7 pts		 Correct verb position, cases, tenses. Small mistakes are OK at B2. Mistakes that stop people from understanding are not OK.
Aussprache und Intonation
Pronunciation · 4 pts		 Clear speech with a natural melody. A foreign accent is fine. A flat, reading voice is not.

Notice: only 7 of 25 points are grammar. Speaking for a long time, giving your opinion, and sounding alive are worth more than zero mistakes.

The question phase

After each talk, the other candidate asks questions. This is scored — for both of you.

  • Ask open W-questions, never yes/no questions. „Hat es dir gefallen?“ gives your partner almost nothing to say. „Was war für dich am schwierigsten?“ gives them thirty seconds.
  • Two or three questions is enough. Listen while your partner speaks. Build a question from something they really said. That shows you understood.
  • When you answer, do not give one word. Answer, then add a reason or an example. Two or three sentences per answer.
04

Ready-made phrases (Redemittel)

These are the fixed parts of your talk. Learn them until they come out without thinking. Then your mind is free for the story.

Starting your talk
Ich möchte euch heute von … erzählen.
Today I'd like to tell you about … — the safest, most natural opening.
Ich habe mich für das Thema „…“ entschieden, weil es mir persönlich viel bedeutet.
I chose the topic "…" because it means a lot to me personally.
Mir fällt dazu sofort … ein.
The first thing I think of is … — this sounds natural, which is the effect you want.
Setting the scene
Das war vor etwa drei Jahren, als ich noch in … gewohnt habe.
That was about three years ago, when I was still living in …
Ich war damals ungefähr zwanzig Jahre alt.
I was about twenty years old at the time.
Ich bin zusammen mit meiner Schwester dorthin gefahren.
I went there together with my sister.
Telling the story — adding colour
Am Anfang war es ehrlich gesagt ziemlich schwierig.
At the beginning it was honestly quite difficult. — "ehrlich gesagt" makes you sound like a person, not a textbook.
Was mich besonders beeindruckt hat, war …
What impressed me particularly was …
Ich erinnere mich noch genau daran, wie …
I still remember exactly how …
Plötzlich hat sich alles verändert.
Suddenly everything changed.
Besonders schwierig war für mich, dass …
What was particularly difficult for me was that …
Giving your opinion — this part brings the points
Rückblickend würde ich sagen, dass …
Looking back, I would say that … — the most useful phrase in this part.
Diese Erfahrung war für mich deshalb so wichtig, weil …
This experience was so important to me because …
Ich habe dabei nicht nur …, sondern vor allem … gelernt.
In doing so I learned not only …, but above all … — this structure always sounds strong.
Seitdem sehe ich vieles mit anderen Augen.
Since then I see many things differently.
Closing and handing over
Deshalb kann ich jedem nur empfehlen, …
That's why I can only recommend to everyone that …
Das war meine Erfahrung. Möchtest du etwas dazu wissen?
That was my experience. Would you like to know anything about it? — a clean, natural way to hand over.
Asking your partner questions
Was hat dir daran am besten gefallen?
What did you like best about it?
Wie hast du dich in diesem Moment gefühlt?
How did you feel at that moment?
Was würdest du heute anders machen?
What would you do differently today?
Du hast gerade … erwähnt — kannst du das genauer erklären?
You just mentioned … — can you explain that in more detail? — this shows you were really listening.
Würdest du das noch einmal machen? Warum (nicht)?
Would you do it again? Why (not)?
Buying yourself time — instead of silence
Das ist eine gute Frage, lass mich kurz überlegen.
That's a good question, let me think for a second.
Wie soll ich das sagen …
How should I put this … — a real filler that native speakers use.
Mir fällt das Wort gerade nicht ein, aber ich meine so etwas wie …
I can't think of the word right now, but I mean something like … — describing around a missing word is better than going silent.
05

Exam tips

Time management

At home
Prepare all seven talksOne keyword sheet per topic. Practise each talk out loud with a timer, until 90 seconds feels normal.
Prep room
Max 4–5 minutes on Part 1Choose your topic, write your keywords, then move on. Parts 2 and 3 need the other 15 minutes much more.
In the talk
Aim for 90 secondsUnder 60 seconds looks like you had nothing to say. Over two minutes, and the examiner may stop you in the middle of a sentence.

What examiners want

  • Your opinion, not only the story. The task is talking about experiences. They want to hear why it was important for you — not only a list of events in order.
  • Continuous speech. About a minute and a half, without long pauses.
  • Natural voice. Sentence melody, stress, a rhythm that sounds like speaking.
  • Real questions and answers — both when you ask and when you answer.

Common mistakes to avoid

Reading a full text from the note paper.
Keywords only. Full sentences cost you points in voice and fluency. Examiners see it at once.
A memorised talk that sounds like reading — same speed, no pauses, no stress.
Learn the plan and the phrases. Keep the middle a little free.
Only the plot: "then we went there, then we ate, then we came back."
Give at least a quarter of your time to Rückblickend würde ich sagen … and why it was important.
Choosing an "impressive" topic when you do not have the words for it.
Choose the topic where you can speak most easily. There are no extra points for a hard topic.
Going silent when a word is missing.
Say it another way, out loud. „Ich meine so etwas wie …“ — talking around a gap is a B2 skill. It gets a good score.
Asking your partner a yes/no question, then having nothing left to say.
Prepare three W-questions that work for any topic. They are in the phrase list above.
Correcting every small slip and breaking your own flow.
Correct a real mistake once, calmly, then continue. Constant self-correction hurts fluency more than the mistake did.

Three small practical tips

  • Record yourself. Phone voice memo, 90 seconds, then listen. You will hear your pauses and flat voice more clearly than you feel them.
  • Pick topics that share a story. A trip, an important experience, and an important person can all come from the same story, with a different focus — three topics for the work of one.
  • Speak to your partner, not the examiners. The task is a conversation between candidates. Eye contact with your partner looks like real communication.

Short review

The format

  • Choose 1 of 7 fixed topics · ~90-second talk · then your partner’s questions
  • Whole part ≈ 5 minutes · 25 of 75 speaking points
  • The self-introduction before this part is not scored
  • 20 minutes prep covers all three speaking parts — use only 4–5 here

The seven topics

  • eine Reise · ein Buch · ein Film · ein Sportereignis · eine Musikveranstaltung · eine wichtige Person · eine wichtige Erfahrung

The plan to remember

  • Einstieg → Rahmen → Kern → Bewertung → Abschluss
  • The Bewertung is what the task really asks for — never skip it

The scoring

  • Ausdrucksfähigkeit 7 · Aufgabenbewältigung 7 · Formale Richtigkeit 7 · Aussprache 4
  • Only 7 of 25 points are grammar — fluency and the task are worth more

The most useful habit

  • Practise out loud with a timer, from keywords. Not silently. Not from a full text.

Before Day 3

If you want to practise: write a keyword sheet for three of the seven topics. Record a 90-second talk for each. Check that at least a quarter of every talk is opinion, not only the story.
