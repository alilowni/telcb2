01

Introduction

Today we cover the first assessed part of the oral exam: Über Erfahrungen sprechen — talking about an experience.

You choose one topic from a fixed list of seven, speak about it for roughly 90 seconds, and then your partner asks you a few questions about it. Later you do the same for your partner: you listen to their talk and ask them questions. Both halves are scored.

Many teachers and YouTube channels call this part „die Präsentation“. That is an informal nickname — the official telc name is Über Erfahrungen sprechen, and it matters, because this is not a formal presentation with structure and statistics. It is a personal story.

Note on the warm-up. Before this part, the examiners will ask you to introduce yourself briefly (Einander kennenlernen / Kontaktaufnahme). In telc B2 this warm-up is not scored. Don't confuse it with telc B1, where introducing yourself is an assessed task.

Why this part matters

  • It is worth 25 of the 75 oral points — one third of your speaking grade.
  • The oral block must independently reach 60%, meaning 45 of 75 points. There is no compensation from the written exam.
  • It is the first impression the two examiners get of your German. A confident, fluent opening changes how they hear everything you say afterwards.
  • Unlike Parts 2 and 3, the content is entirely under your control. You already know the seven possible topics.
02

What we're going to learn today

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  1. Name all seven possible topics and the sub-points that come with each.
  2. Build a five-part structure for a 90-second talk that works for any of them.
  3. Write a keyword note sheet (Stichwortzettel) instead of a script.
  4. Ask your partner good follow-up questions — and answer theirs.
  5. Understand the four criteria examiners score you on, and what each one rewards.

Today's goal in one sentence

Walk out with one prepared talk for each of the seven topics, and a fixed frame you can drop any story into.

You get 20 minutes of preparation before the oral exam, in a separate room, with the task sheets and paper for notes. That 20 minutes has to cover all three speaking parts. Since Part 1 can be prepared at home, spend no more than 4–5 minutes on it in the exam room and give the rest to Parts 2 and 3.

The oral exam is normally taken in pairs (sometimes groups of three) with two licensed examiners. The examiners mostly moderate — they are not your conversation partner. Your partner is.

The seven topics

The list was expanded from five to seven topics in October 2019. Older prep books may still show only five — this is the current set. Each topic comes with sub-points you are meant to touch on.

01
Eine Reise
A trip
Destination, when, the country and its people, sights, why it stayed with you.
02
Ein Buch
A book
Theme, author, what it is about, your opinion, who you'd recommend it to.
03
Ein Film
A film
Plot, actors, genre, your opinion, what impressed you.
04
Ein Sportereignis
A sports event
Which sport, where, who was there, the result, the atmosphere.
05
Eine Musikveranstaltung
A music event
Genre, musicians, venue, audience, how you felt.
06
Eine wichtige Person im Leben
An important person in your life
Who, how you met, when, why they matter to you.
07
Eine wichtige Erfahrung
An important experience
What, when, where, with whom, why it was important.

Prepare all seven. Do not gamble on getting your favourite. Preparing seven 90-second talks sounds like a lot, but they share the same frame and about half the same phrases — realistically it is one evening of work plus repetition.

The five-part frame

Every talk, regardless of topic, follows the same shape:

1 · 10s
Einstieg — announce the topicName your topic and say in one clause why you chose it. This buys you a confident start.
2 · 20s
Rahmen — set the sceneWhen, where, who with, how old you were. Concrete facts, no evaluation yet.
3 · 30s
Kern — the substanceWhat actually happened, what was difficult, what surprised you. This is where the detail lives.
4 · 25s
Bewertung — why it matteredYour evaluation, justified. This is the part candidates skip, and it's the part the task is actually asking for.
5 · 5s
Abschluss — hand overA closing sentence that signals you're done, so nobody has to wonder.

Steps 1, 2 and 5 are almost entirely fixed phrases. Only steps 3 and 4 change with the topic — which is why seven talks is less work than it sounds.

A model talk — Eine wichtige Erfahrung

1 · Einstieg

Ich möchte euch heute von einer Erfahrung erzählen, die mein Leben ziemlich verändert hat: von meinem ersten Auslandssemester in Wien.

2 · Rahmen

Das war vor ungefähr vier Jahren, im Herbst. Ich war damals 21 Jahre alt und hatte vorher noch nie länger als zwei Wochen allein gelebt. Ich bin also mit zwei Koffern angekommen und kannte in der ganzen Stadt keinen einzigen Menschen.

3 · Kern

Am Anfang war es ehrlich gesagt ziemlich hart. In der Universität habe ich zwar alles verstanden, aber im Alltag habe ich mich oft nicht getraut zu sprechen. Besonders schwierig war für mich, dass ich abends allein in meinem Zimmer saß und niemanden hatte, mit dem ich reden konnte. Nach etwa einem Monat bin ich dann einem Laufverein beigetreten — und plötzlich ging alles viel schneller.

4 · Bewertung

Rückblickend würde ich sagen, dass diese Zeit die wichtigste Erfahrung meines Lebens war. Ich habe nämlich nicht nur die Sprache gelernt, sondern vor allem, dass ich mich auf mich selbst verlassen kann. Das hat mir seitdem in vielen Situationen geholfen.

5 · Abschluss

Deshalb kann ich jedem nur empfehlen, einmal für längere Zeit ins Ausland zu gehen. — Das war meine Erfahrung. Hast du dazu Fragen?

In English: the speaker describes their first semester abroad in Vienna at 21, the loneliness of the first month, joining a running club, and concludes that the experience taught them self-reliance. Notice that roughly a third of the talk is evaluation, not events.

You are allowed to invent. Nobody checks whether you really went to Vienna. If a true story is hard to tell in German, tell a simpler one you can tell well. Fluency is scored; biographical accuracy is not.

Notes, not a script

Reading aloud from full sentences destroys your intonation and is immediately obvious to examiners. Your note sheet should look like this:

WIEN — Auslandssemester
vor 4 J. · Herbst · 21 J. alt · 2 Koffer · niemand gekannt
Anfang hart → Uni ok, Alltag nicht · abends allein
nach 1 Monat → Laufverein → alles schneller
Bewertung: wichtigste Erfahrung · nicht nur Sprache, sondern auf mich selbst verlassen
Schluss: jedem empfehlen → ins Ausland

Keywords only. Your eyes should be on your partner and the examiners, not on the paper.

The four scoring criteria

All three speaking parts are scored on the same four criteria, 25 points per part:

CriterionWhat it rewards
Ausdrucksfähigkeit
Range of expression · 7 pts		 Varied vocabulary and sentence structures. Do you have more than one way to say something? Are you using B2 connectors and not just und / dann?
Aufgabenbewältigung
Task completion & fluency · 7 pts		 Did you actually do the task — describe the experience and evaluate it? Did you speak long enough, without long pauses? Did you engage with your partner's questions?
Formale Richtigkeit
Grammatical accuracy · 7 pts		 Correct verb positions, cases, tenses. Small errors are tolerated at B2; errors that break comprehension are not.
Aussprache und Intonation
Pronunciation & intonation · 4 pts		 Clear, comprehensible pronunciation with natural sentence melody. A foreign accent is fine. Monotone reading is not.

Notice that only 7 of 25 points are pure grammar. Speaking at length, evaluating, and sounding alive are worth more than being error-free.

The question phase

After each talk, the other candidate asks questions. This is scored — for both of you.

  • Ask open W-questions, never yes/no questions. „Hat es dir gefallen?“ gives your partner nothing to say. „Was war für dich am schwierigsten?“ gives them thirty seconds.
  • Two or three questions is enough. Listen while your partner speaks and build a question from something they actually said — that shows real comprehension.
  • When answering, don't give one word. Answer, then add a reason or an example. Two to three sentences per answer.
04

Ready-made phrases (Redemittel)

These are the fixed parts of your talk. Learn them until they come out without thinking, so your attention is free for the content.

Starting your talk
Ich möchte euch heute von … erzählen.
Today I'd like to tell you about … — the safest, most natural opening.
Ich habe mich für das Thema „…“ entschieden, weil es mir persönlich viel bedeutet.
I chose the topic "…" because it means a lot to me personally.
Mir fällt dazu sofort … ein.
The first thing that comes to mind is … — sounds spontaneous, which is exactly the effect you want.
Setting the scene
Das war vor etwa drei Jahren, als ich noch in … gewohnt habe.
That was about three years ago, when I was still living in …
Ich war damals ungefähr zwanzig Jahre alt.
I was about twenty years old at the time.
Ich bin zusammen mit meiner Schwester dorthin gefahren.
I went there together with my sister.
Telling the story — adding colour
Am Anfang war es ehrlich gesagt ziemlich schwierig.
At the beginning it was honestly quite difficult. — "ehrlich gesagt" makes you sound like a person, not a textbook.
Was mich besonders beeindruckt hat, war …
What impressed me particularly was …
Ich erinnere mich noch genau daran, wie …
I still remember exactly how …
Plötzlich hat sich alles verändert.
Suddenly everything changed.
Besonders schwierig war für mich, dass …
What was particularly difficult for me was that …
Evaluating — the part that earns the points
Rückblickend würde ich sagen, dass …
Looking back, I would say that … — the single most useful phrase in this part.
Diese Erfahrung war für mich deshalb so wichtig, weil …
This experience was so important to me because …
Ich habe dabei nicht nur …, sondern vor allem … gelernt.
In doing so I learned not only …, but above all … — a structure that always sounds sophisticated.
Seitdem sehe ich vieles mit anderen Augen.
Since then I see many things differently.
Closing and handing over
Deshalb kann ich jedem nur empfehlen, …
That's why I can only recommend to everyone that …
Das war meine Erfahrung. Möchtest du etwas dazu wissen?
That was my experience. Would you like to know anything about it? — a clean, natural hand-over.
Asking your partner questions
Was hat dir daran am besten gefallen?
What did you like best about it?
Wie hast du dich in diesem Moment gefühlt?
How did you feel at that moment?
Was würdest du heute anders machen?
What would you do differently today?
Du hast gerade … erwähnt — kannst du das genauer erklären?
You just mentioned … — can you explain that in more detail? — shows you were really listening.
Würdest du das noch einmal machen? Warum (nicht)?
Would you do it again? Why (not)?
Buying yourself time — instead of silence
Das ist eine gute Frage, lass mich kurz überlegen.
That's a good question, let me think for a second.
Wie soll ich das sagen …
How should I put this … — a real filler that native speakers use.
Mir fällt das Wort gerade nicht ein, aber ich meine so etwas wie …
I can't think of the word right now, but I mean something like … — a paraphrase strategy that scores better than falling silent.
05

Exam tips

Time management

At home
Prepare all seven talksOne keyword sheet per topic. Practise each aloud with a timer until 90 seconds feels natural.
Prep room
Max 4–5 minutes on Part 1Choose your topic, jot your keywords, move on. Parts 2 and 3 need the remaining 15 minutes far more.
In the talk
Aim for 90 secondsUnder 60 seconds looks like you had nothing to say. Over two minutes and the examiner will stop you mid-sentence.

What examiners are looking for

  • Evaluation, not just narration. The task is called talking about experiences — they want to hear why it mattered to you, not a chronological list of events.
  • Continuous speech. A minute and a half without collapsing into pauses.
  • Natural intonation. Sentence melody, stress, a rhythm that sounds like speech.
  • Real engagement in the question phase — both when asking and when answering.

Common mistakes to avoid

Reading a fully written text off the note paper.
Keywords only. It costs you points in intonation and fluency, and examiners spot it instantly.
A memorised talk that sounds recited — same speed, no pauses, no emphasis.
Memorise the frame and the phrases; let the middle stay slightly improvised.
Pure plot summary: "then we went there, then we ate, then we came back."
Give at least a quarter of your time to Rückblickend würde ich sagen … and why it mattered.
Choosing an "impressive" topic you lack vocabulary for.
Choose the topic where you can speak most easily. There are no bonus points for ambition.
Falling silent when a word is missing.
Paraphrase out loud. „Ich meine so etwas wie …“ — describing around a gap is a B2 skill and is scored positively.
Asking your partner a yes/no question, then having nothing left to say.
Prepare three universal W-questions that work for any topic. They're in the phrase list above.
Over-correcting every small slip and breaking your own flow.
Correct a real mistake once, calmly, then move on. Constant self-correction damages fluency more than the error did.

Three quick practical tips

  • Record yourself. Phone voice memo, 90 seconds, listen back. You will hear your pauses and monotone far more clearly than you feel them.
  • Pick topics that overlap. A trip, an important experience and an important person can all be built from the same story with a different emphasis — that's three topics for the price of one.
  • Speak to your partner, not the examiners. The task is a conversation between candidates. Eye contact with your partner reads as natural communication.

Recap

The format

  • Choose 1 of 7 fixed topics · ~90-second talk · then partner's questions
  • Whole part ≈ 5 minutes · worth 25 of 75 oral points
  • The self-introduction beforehand is not scored
  • 20 minutes prep covers all three speaking parts — use only 4–5 here

The seven topics

  • eine Reise · ein Buch · ein Film · ein Sportereignis · eine Musikveranstaltung · eine wichtige Person · eine wichtige Erfahrung

The frame to remember

  • Einstieg → Rahmen → Kern → Bewertung → Abschluss
  • The Bewertung is what the task is really asking for — never skip it

The scoring

  • Ausdrucksfähigkeit 7 · Aufgabenbewältigung 7 · Formale Richtigkeit 7 · Aussprache 4
  • Only 7 of 25 points are grammar — fluency and task handling are worth more

The single most useful habit

  • Practise aloud with a timer, from keywords. Not silently, not from a script.

Before Day 3

If you want to practise: write a keyword sheet for three of the seven topics and record a 90-second talk for each. Check that at least a quarter of every talk is evaluation, not narration.
