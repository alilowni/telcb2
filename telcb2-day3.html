01

Introduction

Today we cover the second part of the oral exam: the Diskussion.

Both candidates receive the same short authentic text — a newspaper or magazine article taking a position on a current, debatable issue. You each summarise it briefly, then discuss it together for about five minutes: your opinion, your reasons, your own experience, and where possible, solutions.

The single most important thing to understand about this part: it is not a test of whether you have interesting opinions. It is a test of whether you can hold a conversation — react to what the other person says, agree, object, come back with a counter-argument. Two candidates who each deliver a brilliant monologue will both score badly.

Why this part matters

  • It is worth 25 of the 75 oral points, and the oral block must reach 45 points on its own.
  • It is the part with the widest topic range — anything from school policy to electric cars — so vocabulary preparation pays off directly.
  • The interaction skills you build here carry straight into Part 3 tomorrow.

Good news about the scoring

You are graded individually, not against your partner. If your partner is weak, that does not drag you down. In fact, helping a struggling partner — asking them a question, picking up a half-finished point — is scored positively, because it demonstrates exactly the interactive competence being tested.

What the text looks like

Expect a short authentic journalistic text — typically 100–200 words — that reports a study, a proposal, or a trend and takes a clear position. It usually contains a built-in conflict: someone argues for something, critics argue against.

The official example in the telc handbook is a study from a social research institute in Hannover arguing in favour of teaching boys and girls in separate schools. That gives you a good sense of the register: serious, everyday-relevant, arguable from both sides.

A sample stimulus text

Beispieltext
Mädchen und Jungen getrennt unterrichten?

Ein Forschungsinstitut aus Hannover kommt zu einem überraschenden Ergebnis: Mädchen und Jungen sollten zumindest in einigen Fächern getrennt unterrichtet werden. In gemischten Klassen, so die Studie, melden sich Mädchen im Physikunterricht deutlich seltener, während Jungen im Deutschunterricht schneller aufgeben. In getrennten Gruppen verschwinde dieser Druck, und beide Seiten trauten sich mehr zu. Kritiker halten dagegen, dass die Schule auf das echte Leben vorbereiten solle — und dort arbeiten Männer und Frauen schließlich jeden Tag zusammen.

In English: a Hannover research institute finds that girls speak up less in physics and boys give up faster in German when classes are mixed; separating them removes that pressure. Critics reply that school should prepare pupils for real life, where men and women work together.

How to read it in three minutes

  • Find the claim. One sentence: who wants what? (The institute wants separated classes.)
  • Find the reason given. (Girls hold back in physics, boys give up in German.)
  • Find the counter-argument. Most texts hand you one for free. (School should mirror real life.)
  • Pick your side and note two arguments plus one personal example. That's your whole preparation.

You do not have to state your real opinion. Choose the side you have more German vocabulary for. Nobody is checking your convictions — they are checking whether you can argue.

The five phases of the discussion

1 · 60s
Zusammenfassung — summarise the textTwo or three sentences each. What is it about, what does the author claim. Don't retell every detail.
2 · 60s
Standpunkt — state your positionSay clearly whether you agree, and give your first reason. Then hand over: „Wie siehst du das?“
3 · 120s
Austausch — the real discussionReact to each other. Agree partially, object, bring examples, counter. This is the core and where the points are.
4 · 45s
Lösungen — move toward solutionsWhat could actually be done? A compromise, a middle path, a condition. Examiners like to hear this.
5 · 15s
Fazit — brief conclusionOne sentence summing up where you both ended up. Optional, but it looks finished.

The argument formula

Every good contribution in this part has the same four steps. Learn it as a reflex:

StepGerman
1 · These
your claim		Ich bin der Meinung, dass getrennte Klassen keine gute Lösung sind.
2 · Begründung
your reason		… weil Kinder gerade in der Schule lernen müssen, miteinander umzugehen.
3 · Beispiel
evidence — usually personal		Bei uns in der Klasse war es zum Beispiel so, dass …
4 · Folgerung / Rückfrage
conclusion or hand-over		Deshalb halte ich das für den falschen Weg. Wie siehst du das?

Four steps takes about twenty seconds to say. That is the right length for one turn. Anything much longer becomes a monologue.

A model exchange

A
In dem Text geht es um die Frage, ob Mädchen und Jungen getrennt unterrichtet werden sollten. Ein Institut aus Hannover ist der Meinung, dass beide Gruppen in getrennten Klassen mutiger wären. Kritiker sagen dagegen, dass die Schule auf das reale Leben vorbereiten muss.Phase 1 — summary in three sentences. Nothing more is needed.
B
Genau. Und ich muss sagen, ich finde die Idee gar nicht so schlecht. Meiner Meinung nach trauen sich Mädchen in gemischten Klassen wirklich weniger — bei uns hat sich in Mathe fast nur die Jungen gemeldet. Wie siehst du das?Position + personal example + hand-over. Short and complete.
A
Da hast du zum Teil recht, das habe ich auch erlebt. Trotzdem sehe ich das anders. Denn wenn wir die Kinder trennen, lösen wir das Problem ja nicht, sondern wir gehen ihm aus dem Weg. Später im Beruf sitzen sie ja wieder zusammen.Partial agreement first, then objection with a reason. This is the highest-scoring move in the whole part.

04

Ready-made phrases (Redemittel)

These are the load-bearing phrases of this part. You will use most of them in every discussion, regardless of topic — which is exactly why they're worth automating.

Summarising the text
In dem Text geht es um die Frage, ob …
The text is about the question of whether … — the cleanest possible opening.
Die Überschrift lautet „…“, und der Autor vertritt die Meinung, dass …
The headline is "…", and the author takes the view that …
Laut einer Studie …
According to a study … — useful whenever the text reports research.
Kritiker halten dagegen, dass …
Critics argue against this that … — lets you show both sides in one sentence.
Giving your opinion
Meiner Meinung nach …
In my opinion … — note the word order: verb comes straight after.
Ich bin der Ansicht, dass …
I take the view that … — a step more formal than "ich denke".
Für mich überwiegen ganz klar die Vorteile.
For me the advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages.
Ich halte das für einen guten / falschen Weg.
I consider that a good / wrong approach.
Justifying — the part that earns points
… und zwar aus einem einfachen Grund: …
… and for one simple reason: … — a strong, natural way to introduce your argument.
Aus eigener Erfahrung kann ich sagen, dass …
From my own experience I can say that … — personal evidence always counts.
Ein gutes Beispiel dafür ist …
A good example of this is …
Man darf dabei nicht vergessen, dass …
One mustn't forget that …
Agreeing — with something added
Da hast du völlig recht.
You're absolutely right there.
So sehe ich das auch, und ich würde sogar noch weiter gehen: …
I see it that way too, and I'd go even further: … — agreement that still adds content.
Das leuchtet mir ein.
That makes sense to me.
Das ist ein gutes Argument.
That's a good argument. — buys you two seconds of thinking time and sounds engaged.
Disagreeing — politely but clearly
the strongest move
Da hast du zum Teil recht. Trotzdem sehe ich das anders, weil …
You're partly right. Nevertheless I see it differently, because … — concede first, then object. This is what a B2 discussion is supposed to sound like.
Ich sehe das etwas anders.
I see that somewhat differently. — soft, safe, always usable.
In diesem Punkt würde ich dir widersprechen.
On this point I would disagree with you.
Das kann ich nicht ganz nachvollziehen. Meinst du wirklich, dass …?
I can't quite follow that. Do you really think that …?
Einerseits stimmt das, andererseits …
On the one hand that's true, on the other hand … — the classic weighing structure.
Keeping the conversation alive
Wie siehst du das?
How do you see it? — end two or three of your turns with this and your interaction score takes care of itself.
Was meinst du dazu?
What do you think about that?
Entschuldige, wenn ich dich kurz unterbreche, aber …
Sorry to interrupt you briefly, but … — polite interruption is a scored skill, not rudeness.
Darf ich dazu noch etwas ergänzen?
May I add something to that?
if your partner is stuck
Und wie ist das eigentlich in deinem Land?
And how is that actually in your country? — rescuing a silent partner is scored positively.
Solutions and conclusion
Vielleicht könnte man beides kombinieren.
Perhaps one could combine both.
Ein möglicher Kompromiss wäre, dass …
A possible compromise would be that …
Man müsste vor allem dafür sorgen, dass …
Above all, one would have to ensure that …
Zusammenfassend kann man sagen, dass wir uns in einem Punkt einig sind: …
To sum up, we agree on one point: …
05

Exam tips

Time management

Prep room
~8 of your 20 minutesRead the text twice, note the claim and the counter-claim, choose your side, write two arguments and one personal example as keywords.
First minute
Summary — keep it shortThree sentences. Candidates who retell the whole text eat up the time they need for arguing.
Throughout
Aim for a 50/50 splitRoughly equal talking time. Turns of about 20 seconds each.
Last minute
Steer toward a solutionEven if you disagree, propose a middle path. It gives the discussion a shape.

What examiners are looking for

  • Genuine interaction — you build on what your partner actually said, using their words.
  • Justified opinions — never a bare claim without a reason.
  • Range of expression — different phrases for agreeing and disagreeing, not „ja, aber“ five times.
  • Balanced participation — you neither dominate nor disappear.
  • Politeness under disagreement — you can object without being blunt.

Common mistakes to avoid

Delivering a two-minute monologue with everything you prepared.
Short turns, then hand over. The interaction is the task.
Retelling the text in detail instead of summarising it.
Three sentences maximum, then straight to your position.
Sitting in silence while your partner talks, waiting for your turn.
Show you're listening — „Genau.“ „Das stimmt.“ „Interessant.“ — then react to their content.
Refusing to take a side: "I agree and I also disagree, it depends."
Pick a position in the prep room and defend it. You can soften later; you cannot argue from nowhere.
Blunt contradiction: „Nein, das ist falsch.“
Concede, then object: „Da hast du zum Teil recht, trotzdem …“
Talking to the examiners instead of your partner.
Face your partner. The examiners are observers, not participants.
Panicking because the topic is unfamiliar.
You are not judged on expertise. „Damit kenne ich mich nicht besonders gut aus, aber ich stelle mir vor, dass …“ is a perfectly good B2 sentence.

Three quick practical tips

  • Prepare three universal arguments that fit almost any topic: money/cost, time, and effect on health or on children. You can attach them to nearly anything.
  • Practise the concede-then-object move until it's automatic. It is the single phrase pattern that most distinguishes a B2 speaker from a B1 one.
  • Read one German opinion piece a week — a short Pro-und-Contra column is ideal. You are collecting arguments, not just vocabulary.

Recap

The format

  • Both candidates read the same short controversial newspaper text
  • Brief summary, then ~5 minutes of discussion · 25 of 75 oral points
  • Scored individually — a weak partner does not lower your score
  • Helping a struggling partner is scored positively

The five phases

  • Zusammenfassung → Standpunkt → Austausch → Lösungen → Fazit

The argument formula

  • These → Begründung → Beispiel → Folgerung / Rückfrage
  • About twenty seconds per turn, then hand over

The one phrase to over-learn

  • „Da hast du zum Teil recht. Trotzdem sehe ich das anders, weil …“
  • Concede, then object — this is what B2 discussion sounds like

The single most useful habit

  • End your turns with a question. „Wie siehst du das?“ forces the interaction the examiners are grading.

Before Day 4

If you want to practise: take three topics from the list above, and for each, write two arguments for and two against in German. Practise defending whichever side you find harder.
