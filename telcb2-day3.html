01

Introduction

Today we look at the second part of the speaking exam: the Diskussion.

Both candidates get the same short real text — a newspaper or magazine article. It takes a side on a current topic people can argue about. Each of you summarises it in a short way. Then you discuss it together for about five minutes: your opinion, your reasons, your own experience, and if you can, solutions.

The most important thing to understand: this is not a test of “interesting opinions”. It is a test of conversation. You must react to what the other person says. You agree. You object. You come back with a counter-argument. Two people who each give a brilliant speech, one after the other, will both get a weak score.

Why this part is important

  • It is 25 of the 75 speaking points. The speaking block must reach 45 points by itself.
  • It has the widest topic range — from school rules to electric cars. So learning topic words helps you a lot.
  • The conversation skills you learn here also help you in Part 3 tomorrow.

Good news about the scoring

You are scored one by one, not against your partner. If your partner is weak, that does not pull you down. In fact, helping a partner who is stuck — asking them a question, picking up a half-finished idea — gets a good score. Because that is exactly the conversation skill they want to see.

What the text looks like

Expect a short newspaper-style text — often 100–200 words. It reports a study, a plan, or a trend. It takes a clear side. It usually has a conflict inside: someone argues for something, other people argue against.

The official example in the telc handbook is a study from a research institute in Hannover. It argues for teaching boys and girls in separate schools. That shows the style: serious, about everyday life, and you can argue both sides.

A sample stimulus text

Beispieltext
Mädchen und Jungen getrennt unterrichten?

Ein Forschungsinstitut aus Hannover kommt zu einem überraschenden Ergebnis: Mädchen und Jungen sollten zumindest in einigen Fächern getrennt unterrichtet werden. In gemischten Klassen, so die Studie, melden sich Mädchen im Physikunterricht deutlich seltener, während Jungen im Deutschunterricht schneller aufgeben. In getrennten Gruppen verschwinde dieser Druck, und beide Seiten trauten sich mehr zu. Kritiker halten dagegen, dass die Schule auf das echte Leben vorbereiten solle — und dort arbeiten Männer und Frauen schließlich jeden Tag zusammen.

In English: a Hannover research institute finds that girls speak up less in physics and boys give up faster in German when classes are mixed; separating them removes that pressure. Critics reply that school should prepare pupils for real life, where men and women work together.

How to read it in three minutes

  • Find the claim. One sentence: who wants what? (The institute wants separate classes.)
  • Find the reason they give. (Girls hold back in physics, boys give up in German.)
  • Find the counter-argument. Most texts give you one for free. (School should look like real life.)
  • Pick your side. Note two arguments plus one personal example. That is your whole preparation.

You do not have to say your real opinion. Choose the side where you have more German words. Nobody checks what you really believe. They check if you can argue.

The five phases of the discussion

1 · 60s
Zusammenfassung — summarise the textTwo or three sentences each. What is it about? What does the writer claim? Do not retell every detail.
2 · 60s
Standpunkt — say your positionSay clearly if you agree. Give your first reason. Then hand over: „Wie siehst du das?“
3 · 120s
Austausch — the real discussionReact to each other. Agree in part, object, give examples, counter. This is the core. This is where the points are.
4 · 45s
Lösungen — move toward solutionsWhat could people really do? A compromise, a middle way, a condition. Examiners like to hear this.
5 · 15s
Fazit — a short endingOne sentence about where you both ended. Optional, but it looks finished.

The argument formula

Every good turn in this part has the same four steps. Learn it like a habit:

StepGerman
1 · These
your claim		Ich bin der Meinung, dass getrennte Klassen keine gute Lösung sind.
2 · Begründung
your reason		… weil Kinder gerade in der Schule lernen müssen, miteinander umzugehen.
3 · Beispiel
an example — often from your life		Bei uns in der Klasse war es zum Beispiel so, dass …
4 · Folgerung / Rückfrage
ending or a question to your partner		Deshalb halte ich das für den falschen Weg. Wie siehst du das?

Four steps take about twenty seconds to say. That is a good length for one turn. Much longer, and it becomes a speech, not a conversation.

A model exchange

A
In dem Text geht es um die Frage, ob Mädchen und Jungen getrennt unterrichtet werden sollten. Ein Institut aus Hannover ist der Meinung, dass beide Gruppen in getrennten Klassen mutiger wären. 04

Ready-made phrases (Redemittel)

These are the main phrases of this part. You will use most of them in every discussion, on any topic. That is why they are worth learning until they feel automatic.

Summarising the text
In dem Text geht es um die Frage, ob …
The text is about the question of whether … — the cleanest opening.
Die Überschrift lautet „…“, und der Autor vertritt die Meinung, dass …
The headline is "…", and the author takes the view that …
Laut einer Studie …
According to a study … — useful when the text talks about research.
Kritiker halten dagegen, dass …
Critics argue against this that … — this lets you show both sides in one sentence.
Giving your opinion
Meiner Meinung nach …
In my opinion … — note the word order: verb comes straight after.
Ich bin der Ansicht, dass …
I take the view that … — a bit more formal than "ich denke".
Für mich überwiegen ganz klar die Vorteile.
For me the advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages.
Ich halte das für einen guten / falschen Weg.
I consider that a good / wrong approach.
Giving a reason — this part brings the points
… und zwar aus einem einfachen Grund: …
… and for one simple reason: … — a strong, natural way to start your argument.
Aus eigener Erfahrung kann ich sagen, dass …
From my own experience I can say that … — personal evidence always counts.
Ein gutes Beispiel dafür ist …
A good example of this is …
Man darf dabei nicht vergessen, dass …
One mustn't forget that …
Agreeing — with something added
Da hast du völlig recht.
You're absolutely right there.
So sehe ich das auch, und ich würde sogar noch weiter gehen: …
I see it that way too, and I'd go even further: … — agreement that still adds content.
Das leuchtet mir ein.
That makes sense to me.
Das ist ein gutes Argument.
That's a good argument. — this buys you two seconds to think, and it sounds engaged.
Disagreeing — politely but clearly
the strongest move
Da hast du zum Teil recht. Trotzdem sehe ich das anders, weil …
You're partly right. Nevertheless I see it differently, because … — first accept a bit, then object. This is what a B2 discussion should sound like.
Ich sehe das etwas anders.
I see that a bit differently. — soft, safe, always usable.
In diesem Punkt würde ich dir widersprechen.
On this point I would disagree with you.
Das kann ich nicht ganz nachvollziehen. Meinst du wirklich, dass …?
I can't quite follow that. Do you really think that …?
Einerseits stimmt das, andererseits …
On the one hand that's true, on the other hand … — the classic “both sides” structure.
Keeping the conversation alive
Wie siehst du das?
How do you see it? — end two or three of your turns with this, and your interaction score takes care of itself.
Was meinst du dazu?
What do you think about that?
Entschuldige, wenn ich dich kurz unterbreche, aber …
Sorry to interrupt you briefly, but … — a polite interruption is a scored skill, not rudeness.
Darf ich dazu noch etwas ergänzen?
May I add something to that?
if your partner is stuck
Und wie ist das eigentlich in deinem Land?
And how is that actually in your country? — helping a silent partner gets a good score.
Solutions and conclusion
Vielleicht könnte man beides kombinieren.
Perhaps one could combine both.
Ein möglicher Kompromiss wäre, dass …
A possible compromise would be that …
Man müsste vor allem dafür sorgen, dass …
Above all, one would have to ensure that …
Zusammenfassend kann man sagen, dass wir uns in einem Punkt einig sind: …
To sum up, we agree on one point: …
05

Exam tips

Time management

Prep room
~8 of your 20 minutesRead the text twice. Note the claim and the counter-claim. Choose your side. Write two arguments and one personal example as keywords.
First minute
Summary — keep it shortThree sentences. People who retell the whole text use up the time they need for arguing.
Throughout
Aim for a 50/50 splitAbout the same talking time. Turns of about 20 seconds each.
Last minute
Steer toward a solutionEven if you disagree, suggest a middle way. It gives the discussion a shape.

What examiners want

  • Real conversation — you build on what your partner actually said, using their words.
  • Opinions with reasons — never a claim with no reason.
  • Range of language — different phrases for agreeing and disagreeing, not „ja, aber“ five times.
  • Balanced talking time — you neither take over nor disappear.
  • Polite disagreement — you can object without being hard or rude.

Common mistakes to avoid

Giving a two-minute speech with everything you prepared.
Short turns, then hand over. The conversation is the task.
Retelling the text in detail instead of summarising it.
Three sentences maximum, then straight to your position.
Sitting in silence while your partner talks, waiting for your turn.
Show you are listening — „Genau.“ „Das stimmt.“ „Interessant.“ — then react to what they said.
Refusing to take a side: "I agree and I also disagree, it depends."
Pick a position in the prep room and defend it. You can soften later. You cannot argue from nowhere.
Hard contradiction: „Nein, das ist falsch.“
First accept a bit, then object: „Da hast du zum Teil recht, trotzdem …“
Talking to the examiners instead of your partner.
Face your partner. The examiners watch. They are not in the conversation.
Panicking because the topic is new to you.
You are not scored on expert knowledge. „Damit kenne ich mich nicht besonders gut aus, aber ich stelle mir vor, dass …“ is a perfectly good B2 sentence.

Three small practical tips

  • Prepare three arguments that fit almost any topic: money/cost, time, and effect on health or on children. You can attach them to almost anything.
  • Practise “accept, then object” until it feels automatic. This is the phrase pattern that most clearly shows B2, not B1.
  • Read one German opinion text a week — a short Pro-und-Contra column is ideal. You collect arguments, not only words.

Short review

The format

  • Both candidates read the same short newspaper text people can argue about
  • Short summary, then ~5 minutes of discussion · 25 of 75 speaking points
  • Scored one by one — a weak partner does not lower your score
  • Helping a partner who is stuck gets a good score

The five phases

  • Zusammenfassung → Standpunkt → Austausch → Lösungen → Fazit

The argument formula

  • These → Begründung → Beispiel → Folgerung / Rückfrage
  • About twenty seconds per turn, then hand over

The one phrase to over-learn

  • „Da hast du zum Teil recht. Trotzdem sehe ich das anders, weil …“
  • First accept a bit, then object — this is what B2 discussion sounds like

The most useful habit

  • End your turns with a question. „Wie siehst du das?“ forces the conversation the examiners are scoring.
