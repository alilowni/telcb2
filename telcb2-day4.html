01

Introduction

Today we cover the final part of the oral exam: Gemeinsam etwas planen — planning something together.

You and your partner get a short scenario with a few prompts, and about five minutes to plan the thing described: a party, a trip, an office event, a gift, a school festival. You make suggestions, react to each other's ideas, negotiate where you disagree, and arrive at a plan you both accept.

The crucial difference from yesterday. In the discussion, disagreement is the point — you can end five minutes apart. Here, disagreement is only a step on the way. The task is not complete unless you decide. Sometimes that means giving in, and giving in gracefully is a scored skill, not a loss.

Why this part matters

  • It is the last 25 of the 75 oral points — and the oral block must reach 45 on its own to pass.
  • It is the part where candidates most often run out of prompts — they plan the first bullet point beautifully and never reach the rest.
  • It is also the easiest part to sound natural in, because the German you need is everyday German: suggesting, agreeing, proposing a compromise.

What the task looks like

You get a short situation — two or three sentences — plus a handful of prompts to cover. The official example in the telc handbook is a group of senior citizens (60 and over) who want to travel through Germany, Austria or Switzerland; the candidates are asked to help them plan the trip and put together a programme.

Here is a typical task in the same style:

Beispielaufgabe
Abschied von einer Kollegin

Ihre Kollegin Frau Berger verlässt nach zwölf Jahren die Firma. Sie und Ihr Partner / Ihre Partnerin sollen für sie eine kleine Abschiedsfeier organisieren. Planen Sie gemeinsam.

  • Wann und wo soll die Feier stattfinden?
  • Wen laden Sie ein?
  • Essen und Getränke
  • Geschenk
  • Wer übernimmt welche Aufgaben?

In English: a colleague is leaving after twelve years; plan a farewell party — when and where, who to invite, food and drink, a present, and who does what.

The point-by-point method

This is the whole technique, and it is the difference between a controlled performance and a mess:

  1. Frame the task in one sentence — „Also, wir sollen eine Abschiedsfeier für Frau Berger organisieren.“
  2. Take the prompts in order. Say which one you're on: „Fangen wir mit dem Termin an.“
  3. Suggest → react → decide. Settle that point before moving to the next.
  4. Mark the transition — „Gut, das hätten wir. Kommen wir zum Geschenk.“
  5. Summarise the whole plan at the end.

With five prompts and five minutes, you have roughly 50 seconds per point. That is enough for two suggestions and a decision — and not enough for a debate. Keep the small decisions fast so the time is spread evenly.

Pick your battles. Disagree on one or two points to show you can negotiate — examiners want to hear a compromise being reached. Then agree quickly on the rest. Fighting over every detail means you'll never reach the last prompt.

A model exchange

A
Also, wir sollen ja eine kleine Abschiedsfeier für Frau Berger organisieren. Fangen wir vielleicht mit dem Termin an — was meinst du?Frames the task, names the first point, hands over. Ten seconds.
B
Gute Idee. Ich würde vorschlagen, dass wir die Feier an ihrem letzten Arbeitstag machen, also am Freitagnachmittag. Dann müssen die Kollegen nicht extra noch einmal kommen.Suggestion plus a reason. Never suggest without justifying — it's an easy point in "Ausdrucksfähigkeit".
A
Einverstanden, das ist praktisch. Und wo? Ich hätte an das Restaurant gegenüber gedacht.Quick agreement on a small point, then straight to the next question. This is how you stay on time.
B
Hm, ehrlich gesagt bin ich mir da nicht sicher. Ein Restaurant ist ziemlich teuer, und nicht alle Kollegen können mitkommen. Wie wäre es, wenn wir es einfach im großen Besprechungsraum machen?Polite objection with two reasons, then an alternative. Notice: never just "nein".
A
Da hast du recht, die Kosten sind ein gutes Argument. Können wir uns darauf einigen, dass wir im Besprechungsraum feiern und dafür beim Essen etwas mehr ausgeben?Concedes, then proposes a compromise. This exact move is what the examiners are listening for.
B
Ja, so machen wir das. Dann halten wir fest: Freitagnachmittag, Besprechungsraum. Kommen wir zum Geschenk — hast du eine Idee?Confirms the decision out loud and marks the transition to the next prompt.
A
Sie fotografiert doch so gern. Wir könnten von allen Kollegen Geld sammeln und ihr einen Gutschein für einen Fotokurs schenken. Was hältst du davon?A concrete, specific idea. Detail like this scores far better than "vielleicht ein Buch".
B
Das gefällt mir sehr gut. Ich kann das Geld einsammeln, wenn du dich um das Essen kümmerst. — Fassen wir also zusammen: Die Feier ist am Freitag um 16 Uhr im Besprechungsraum, es gibt Fingerfood und Getränke, und wir schenken ihr einen Gutschein für einen Fotokurs.Divides the tasks, then closes with a full summary. A clean, finished ending.

Scenarios that come back

As with the discussion topics, these come from prep schools and past candidates rather than an official telc list — reliable patterns, not guarantees.

Work & colleagues

Trips & free time
eine Reise für eine Seniorengruppe (the official example) · Wochenendausflug · Städtereise · Wanderwochenende · Grillfest
Community, school & charity

Notice the pattern: almost every scenario needs the same decisions — when, where, who, what it costs, what's on the programme, who does what. Prepare vocabulary for those six questions and you're covered for nearly any scenario.

04

Ready-made phrases (Redemittel)

This part has the most formulaic language of all three speaking tasks — which is good news. Automate these and most of your attention is free for the content.

Opening the planning
Also, wir sollen ja … organisieren. Fangen wir vielleicht mit … an?
So, we're supposed to organise … Shall we start with …? — the perfect opening: it frames the task and takes the lead without dominating.
Sollen wir der Reihe nach vorgehen?
Shall we go through it point by point? — signals structure immediately.
Making suggestions
Ich würde vorschlagen, dass wir …
I would suggest that we … — Konjunktiv II, polite, safe.
Wie wäre es, wenn wir … ?
How would it be if we …? — the most natural-sounding suggestion in German.
Wir könnten auch … , das hätte den Vorteil, dass …
We could also …, that would have the advantage that … — suggestion plus justification in one sentence.
Ich hätte da eine Idee: …
I have an idea: …
Was hältst du davon, wenn wir … ?
What do you think about us …? — suggestion and hand-over together.
Reacting positively
Das ist eine gute Idee, das machen wir so.
That's a good idea, let's do it that way.
Einverstanden.
Agreed. — short, decisive, moves you forward.
Da bin ich ganz deiner Meinung.
I completely agree with you there.
Das gefällt mir gut, aber lass uns überlegen, ob …
I like that, but let's consider whether … — agreement that still adds something.
Objecting — and offering an alternative
the key move
Ehrlich gesagt bin ich mir da nicht sicher, weil … Wie wäre es stattdessen, wenn wir … ?
To be honest I'm not sure about that, because … How about instead if we …? — never object without offering something.
Das könnte schwierig werden, denn …
That could be difficult, because …
Ich würde lieber … , und zwar deshalb, weil …
I would rather …, and for this reason: …
Meinst du nicht, dass das zu teuer / zu kompliziert wird?
Don't you think that will be too expensive / too complicated?
Compromising and deciding
Können wir uns darauf einigen, dass … ?
Can we agree that …? — the single most valuable sentence in this part.
Wie wäre es mit einem Kompromiss: …
How about a compromise: …
Gut, dann halten wir fest: …
Good, so let's note down: … — confirms the decision out loud, which examiners hear as task completion.
Da gebe ich dir recht, machen wir es so.
I'll grant you that, let's do it that way. — conceding well is a skill, not a defeat.
Moving to the next point
Gut, das hätten wir. Kommen wir zum nächsten Punkt: …
Good, that's settled. Let's move on to the next point: …
Was das Essen angeht — hast du eine Idee?
As far as the food is concerned — do you have an idea?
Eine Sache haben wir noch nicht besprochen: …
There's one thing we haven't discussed yet: … — perfect if you notice a missed prompt near the end.
Dividing tasks and closing
Ich kann mich um das Essen kümmern, wenn du die Einladungen übernimmst.
I can take care of the food if you handle the invitations.
Fassen wir noch einmal zusammen: …
Let's summarise once more: … — the ideal final move of the whole oral exam.
Ich glaube, damit haben wir alles geplant.
I think we've planned everything now.

Exam tips

Time management

Prep room
~5 of your 20 minutesNote one concrete idea next to each prompt. Concrete beats clever: "Gutschein für einen Fotokurs" is better than "ein schönes Geschenk".
First 15s
Frame and startName the task, name the first prompt, hand over.
~50s each
One prompt at a timeTwo suggestions, one decision, move on. Watch the clock against the number of prompts left.
Last 20s
Summarise the planEven if you haven't finished everything, a summary makes it sound complete.

What examiners are looking for

  • All the prompts covered — this is the criterion candidates most often fail.
  • Real decisions, stated out loud: „Gut, dann halten wir fest …“
  • Negotiation — at least one point where you disagreed and found a compromise.
  • Balanced turns — neither of you plans the whole thing alone.
  • Justified suggestions — every idea comes with a short reason.

Common mistakes to avoid

Spending three minutes on the first prompt and never reaching the rest.
Roughly 50 seconds per point. Decide, mark the transition, move on.
Agreeing with absolutely everything: "Ja. Gute Idee. Ja, genau. Auch gut."
Object at least once with a reason and an alternative. Pure agreement gives you nothing to demonstrate.
Arguing to the end and never deciding.
The task is planen, not diskutieren. If neither side gives way, propose a compromise or simply concede.
Planning everything alone while your partner listens.
After every suggestion: „Was meinst du?“ A planning task done solo cannot score well.
Vague ideas: „Wir machen etwas Schönes und essen etwas.“
Be specific — a time, a place, a price, a real gift. Detail is what you're being scored on.
Ending abruptly when time runs out.
Watch for the examiner's signal and get your summary in: „Fassen wir zusammen …“
Slipping into Sie with your partner.
Candidates normally use du with each other. Agree on it in the prep room if you get the chance.

Three quick practical tips

  • Memorise the six questions — Wann? Wo? Wer? Was kostet es? Was machen wir? Wer übernimmt was? They fit almost every scenario and will rescue you if the prompts are vague.
  • Use the prep room to talk to your partner if it's allowed. Agreeing on du and who starts removes a lot of friction.
  • Plan one disagreement in advance. Decide that you'll object on the location or the budget. It guarantees you demonstrate negotiation.

Recap

The format

  • Plan something with your partner in ~5 minutes · 25 of 75 oral points
  • A short scenario plus prompts — you must cover them all
  • Unlike the discussion, you must reach an actual agreement

The method

  • Frame → prompt by prompt → suggest, react, decide → mark the transition → summarise
  • Roughly 50 seconds per prompt
  • Disagree on one or two points; agree quickly on the rest

The six questions that fit any scenario

  • Wann? · Wo? · Wer? · Was kostet es? · Was machen wir? · Wer übernimmt was?

The two phrases to over-learn

  • „Können wir uns darauf einigen, dass …?“ — reaching agreement
  • „Gut, das hätten wir. Kommen wir zu …“ — keeping to time

The single most useful habit

  • Say the decision out loud before moving on. If the examiners never hear a decision, they cannot credit one.

The course, in one page

Day 1 · Writing

One letter, choice of two, 150+ words in 30 minutes. Six blocks: Betreff → Anrede → Einleitung → Hauptteil → Forderung → Schlussformel. Handle three Leitpunkte properly rather than four badly. Two of the three criteria reward structure, not grammar.

Day 2 · Über Erfahrungen sprechen

One of seven fixed topics, ~90 seconds, then questions. Einstieg → Rahmen → Kern → Bewertung → Abschluss. Keywords, never a script. Prepare all seven at home.

Day 3 · Diskussion

Summarise in three sentences, take a side, then really talk. These → Begründung → Beispiel → Rückfrage, about twenty seconds per turn. Concede, then object.

Day 4 · Gemeinsam etwas planen

Point by point, ~50 seconds each, decide out loud, summarise at the end. One negotiated compromise, quick agreement elsewhere.

The numbers to keep in mind

300 points total. Written block 225, oral block 75. You need 60% in each block separately — 135 and 45 — and they do not compensate each other. Only 7 of every 25 oral points are pure grammar.
